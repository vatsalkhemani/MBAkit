## Model output

## Your STAR Story

**Situation:**
At my company, our sales performance was underperforming against quarterly targets and required a strategic intervention to reverse the trend.

**Task:**
I took ownership of leading a cross-functional initiative to identify the primary blockers in our sales process and implement a targeted improvement plan.

**Action:**
- [I analyzed our sales funnel data to identify the specific stage where the highest volume of prospects were dropping off.]
- [I conducted interviews with [X number of] sales representatives to understand the qualitative friction points they faced during client calls.]
- [I developed a new [process/tool/training module] to address these gaps, choosing this approach over [alternative option] because it prioritized immediate impact on conversion rates.]
- [I managed a team of [number] stakeholders, navigating pushback regarding [specific challenge or disagreement] by presenting a data-backed pilot plan.]
- [I monitored the implementation closely, making real-time adjustments to the process based on weekly performance metrics.]

**Result:**
The project resulted in a [X]% increase in sales conversion, exceeding our initial target of [Y]%. This taught me that effective leadership requires balancing data-driven insights with the practical feedback of the team on the ground.

---

**Strength check:**
- The structure correctly isolates your personal responsibility (Task) from the broader company context (Situation).
- The "Action" section is set up to showcase your decision-making, which is the core of a strong leadership story.

**Gap flags:**
- **Missing Specifics:** You need to provide the "What" and "How." What was the specific sales problem? What was the "targeted intervention"?
- **Quantification:** You need to provide actual percentages or revenue figures. If you don't have exact numbers, estimate based on your best recollection (e.g., "increased conversion by 15%").
- **Decision Point:** The current action plan is a template. You need to fill in the bracketed info to show *why* you made the choices you did.
- **Pushback:** Leadership stories require a moment of friction. You need to define what the disagreement was (e.g., budget, timeline, or strategy) and how you resolved it.

**Competency tags:** "Tell me about a time you led a team through a challenge," "Tell me about a time you had to influence others to change a process," "Tell me about a time you improved a business outcome."

**Likely follow-ups an interviewer would ask:**
1. "What was the most significant piece of data you found, and how did it change your original plan?"
2. "Tell me about a time you faced pushback from a team member during this project. How did you handle it?"
3. "If you had to do this project again, what is one thing you would do differently?"

**Alternative framings:** This story also works for **Analytical Problem Solving** if you emphasize the data-gathering process in the first two bullets of the Action section.
