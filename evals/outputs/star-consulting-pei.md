## Your STAR Story

**Situation:**
During a telco merger, our team of four was tasked with consolidating two network-operations organizations. The workstream lead’s proposed operating model was based on top-down assumptions that contradicted the operational realities I was hearing from frontline staff.

**Task:**
As the most junior team member, I needed to challenge the flawed model and ensure the final recommendation was grounded in ground-truth data, despite initial pushback from leadership.

**Action:**
- Conducted 15 independent interviews with field operators over one week to map actual maintenance workflows and identify the disconnects in our current model.
- Synthesized these insights into a quantitative counter-model over the weekend, comparing the lead’s theoretical throughput against the reality of operator capacity.
- Presented the findings to my manager on Monday morning, framing the argument not as a personal disagreement, but as a risk-mitigation strategy to prevent future operational bottlenecks.
- Secured the manager’s support to present the model to the partner, where I walked through the specific data points that proved the original assumptions were unsustainable.
- Navigated the partner's skepticism by demonstrating how the new model protected the client from excessive turnover, given their already high attrition rates.

**Result:**
The partner adopted my model, resulting in a more sustainable organizational structure that saved ~40 FTE roles compared to the original plan. This taught me that at junior levels, data beats hierarchy, but influence requires the rigor to "do the work" and bridge the gap between frontline reality and leadership strategy.

**Strength check:**
- Excellent "Personal Impact" narrative; it clearly shows you taking initiative despite being the most junior person.
- The "data over hierarchy" lesson is a classic, high-impact takeaway for consulting interviews.
- You clearly articulated the "why" behind the change (attrition/sustainability).

**Gap flags:**
- **Specifics of the Model:** The "counter-model" is currently a black box. Be prepared to explain *what* the model measured (e.g., "I calculated cost-per-ticket based on actual maintenance time rather than estimated time").
- **The "Brushed Off" moment:** You mention being brushed off twice. In an interview, keep this brief; focus more on how you *prepared* to overcome the skepticism rather than complaining about the lack of initial buy-in.
- **Quantification:** You mentioned saving 40 FTE roles. If possible, estimate the dollar value or the percentage of the total workforce this represented to add scale.

**Competency tags:** Personal Impact, Influencing Others, Dealing with Ambiguity, Problem Solving.

**Likely follow-ups an interviewer would ask:**
1. "How did you handle the potential friction with the workstream lead after your model was adopted?"
2. "Walk me through the specific logic of your counter-model—what was the biggest variable you found that the lead had missed?"
3. "If the partner had rejected your model even after seeing the data, what would have been your next step?"

**Alternative framings:** This story also works for **Leadership** if you emphasize how you proactively managed the relationship with your manager to gain their sponsorship, rather than going behind their back to the partner.
