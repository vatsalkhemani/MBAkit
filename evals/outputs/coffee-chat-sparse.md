## Coffee Chat Prep

**Quick context:** As an SVP at a major public company, Rachel is likely focused on high-level product strategy, organizational scaling, and balancing guest/host incentives. Since you're an alum, focus on the "big picture" of how the product organization operates rather than tactical recruiting logistics.

**Opening line:** It's great to connect, and I've been thinking a lot about how Airbnb balances the tension between platform standardization and the unique, localized nature of the guest experience.

**Questions to ask:**
1. You've navigated several shifts in how the platform prioritizes host versus guest needs; what's the most counterintuitive lesson you've learned about balancing those two sides of the marketplace? *Why this works: It addresses the core business model of the company and invites her to share a high-level strategic insight rather than a generic answer.*
2. At the SVP level, how do you decide which product bets are worth the risk versus which ones are better left to the core, stable features? *Why this works: It shows you're thinking about the trade-offs inherent in leadership and resource allocation.*
3. When you're looking at the product organization, what’s the biggest gap you see between how we’re taught to build products in business school versus what’s actually required to scale a global platform? *Why this works: It leverages your shared MBA background to get a "real talk" perspective on the limitations of academic frameworks.*
4. As the company has matured, how has the culture of the product team changed to keep the speed of a startup while maintaining the rigor of a public company? *Why this works: It demonstrates an understanding of the typical "growth phase" challenges that senior leaders deal with daily.*
5. If you were back in my shoes, what’s one capability you’d double down on developing that you didn’t realize was critical until you reached the executive level? *Why this works: It’s a sophisticated way to ask for advice that forces her to reflect on her own growth trajectory.*

**If the conversation stalls:** 
1. "How do you personally stay close to the user experience when you're managing such a broad portfolio of products?"
2. "What's a trend in the travel or hospitality space that you think is currently being over-hyped?"

---

**Heads up:** At the SVP level, she isn't the right person to ask about the mechanics of the recruiting pipeline or resume screening — if she wants to help you get hired, she'll offer a referral or a connection to a recruiter herself. Since you're both alumni, skip the "how do I break in" talk and instead focus on demonstrating that you have the strategic maturity to operate at her level.
