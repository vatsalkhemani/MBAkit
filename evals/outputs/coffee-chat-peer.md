## Model output

## Coffee Chat Prep

**Quick context:** Sam is in the trenches of a small, high-stakes team, so he'll value directness and technical literacy. Since you're both Wharton, you can skip the pleasantries and get straight to the trade-offs of the early-stage PM grind versus the structured FAANG path.

**Opening line:** It's great to connect, and I've been weighing the trade-offs between the autonomy of a Series A environment and the structured training of a FAANG rotation.

**Questions to ask:**
1. You're two years out of Wharton and already a founding PM at a Series A shop; what’s one thing about the "founding" part of that title that you didn't fully appreciate until you were actually in the seat? *Why this works: It invites him to share a "reality check" moment that goes beyond the standard startup brochure.*
2. I'm coming from a software engineering background and I'm trying to figure out if I'll miss the technical depth in a Series A role where I might be spread thin across product, ops, and strategy. How have you balanced your technical instincts with the need to move fast on non-technical priorities? *Why this works: It signals that you're self-aware about your own background and looking for a specific, tactical answer.*
3. When you look at your peers who went the FAANG route, what do you think you’re gaining in terms of "product intuition" that they might be missing, and vice versa? *Why this works: It frames the decision as a comparative analysis rather than a request for career advice.*
4. If you had to build a "survival guide" for a first-year MBA joining a team of 18, what are the three skills you’d tell them to double down on before graduation? *Why this works: It forces him to distill his experience into actionable advice without being too generic.*
5. What’s the most frustrating part of the job that you’d never see from the outside looking in? *Why this works: It gives him permission to be honest about the "sucks" part of the job, which builds instant trust.*

**If the conversation stalls:** 
- "What's the biggest 'unknown' your team is currently trying to solve for?"
- "How has your perspective on what makes a 'good' product changed since leaving school?"

---

**Heads up:** Since you're both Wharton, don't spend time on "networking" filler — he knows why you're there. Because he's at an 18-person company, he's likely wearing five hats at once; if he mentions a specific operational fire he's putting out, pivot your questions to ask how he manages that specific type of chaos. Don't frame your FAANG vs. Startup dilemma as "which is better," frame it as "what type of operator am I," as that shows you're taking ownership of your career path.
