## Your STAR Story

**Situation:**
At my 80-person fintech startup, our mobile app’s 7-day retention had plateaued at 22% for six months, despite significant pressure from stakeholders to add new features to drive engagement.

**Task:**
As the growth PM, I was responsible for diagnosing the retention stall and determining whether to prioritize new feature development or optimize the existing user journey.

**Action:**
- Analyzed 30 days of funnel data, identifying that 60% of user drop-off occurred between first-launch and the second session.
- Conducted 12 user interviews over two weeks, which revealed that our onboarding process assumed a level of crypto fluency that alienated our target demographic.
- Navigated pushback from my engineering lead, who argued for prioritizing a referral feature due to its faster implementation time.
- Proposed a compromise to run both initiatives as A/B tests in parallel to let data dictate the roadmap, allowing us to move forward without stalling the team.
- Partnered with one designer to overhaul the onboarding flow over three weeks, simplifying the value proposition for non-technical users.

**Result:**
The new onboarding flow increased 7-day retention from 22% to 34% (+54%), while the referral feature yielded negligible impact. This success established a new quarterly rhythm of data-driven onboarding optimization. This taught me that deep user empathy often exposes flaws that surface-level feature requests fail to address.

**Strength check:**
- Excellent use of data to drive the narrative.
- Clear "I" statements that distinguish your contribution from the team's.
- Strong conflict resolution (the compromise/A/B test approach).

**Gap flags:**
- **"Disagree and Commit" nuance:** While you resolved the conflict via A/B testing, Amazon interviewers will want to know how you handled the *emotional* side of the disagreement. Did you express your dissent clearly before agreeing to the test? Did you support the engineer's referral feature launch even while yours was in development?
- **Quantification of the referral feature:** You noted it "barely moved the needle." If you have the specific percentage (e.g., +0.5% retention), add it to show you tracked both experiments rigorously.
- **Decision rationale:** Why did you choose the referral feature as the specific "test" to run alongside your onboarding work? Did you have a backup plan if the A/B test results were inconclusive?

**Competency tags:** Disagree and Commit, Bias for Action, Dive Deep, Customer Obsession.

2. "If the A/B test results had been inconclusive, how would you have decided which project to prioritize?"

**Alternative framings:** This story also works for **"Dive Deep"** if you emphasize the data-sourcing process, or **"Customer Obsession"** if you focus heavily on the specific insights gained from the 12 user interviews and how you translated those into product requirements.
