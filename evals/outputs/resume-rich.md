# resume-rich

**Tool:** resume
**Mode:** rich
**Label:** Career-switcher bullets (military) with some metrics, targeting consulting
**Latency:** 5382ms

---

## User message sent

```
Sharpen these resume bullets:

- Platoon leader for 40 soldiers during 9-month deployment, maintained 100% equipment readiness
- Coordinated logistics for battalion-wide training exercise involving 600 personnel across 3 locations
- Wrote standard operating procedures adopted by 2 other units

Target role/industry: Consulting (McKinsey/BCG/Bain)
Goal: MBB consulting summer internship

Analyze each bullet against the diagnosis checklist. Provide all three versions for each. Follow the output format exactly.
```

---

## Model output

### Bullet 1
**Original:** Platoon leader for 40 soldiers during 9-month deployment, maintained 100% equipment readiness

**Diagnosis:**
- WEAK VERB: "Platoon leader" is a title, not an action verb. "Maintained" is a low-impact verb that suggests status quo rather than improvement.
- BURIED LEAD: The scope of leadership (40 people) is great, but it is buried at the end of the clause.
- NO NUMBERS: The 40 and 100% are excellent, but you need to frame them in terms of operational efficiency or asset value to appeal to MBB.

**Version 1 (Safe improvement):** Led a 40-person team during a 9-month deployment, ensuring 100% equipment readiness for all mission-critical assets.
*What changed: Converted the title into an action verb and tightened the phrasing to focus on leadership responsibility.*

**Version 2 (Stronger rewrite):** Directed a 40-person team through a 9-month deployment, achieving 100% equipment readiness across $[X]M in assets to ensure 24/7 operational capability.
*What changed: Added a placeholder for asset value to demonstrate fiscal responsibility and scaled the impact of the readiness metric.*

**Version 3 (Strongest):** Commanded a 40-person unit during a 9-month deployment, maintaining 100% equipment availability for $[X]M in assets while optimizing maintenance workflows to reduce downtime by [Y]%.
*What changed: Used "Commanded" to emphasize authority and added a process improvement component to show you don't just maintain standards, you optimize them.*

---

### Bullet 2
**Original:** Coordinated logistics for battalion-wide training exercise involving 600 personnel across 3 locations

**Diagnosis:**
- WEAK VERB: "Coordinated" is a non-committal verb that undersells the complexity of the task.
- NO NUMBERS: While you have personnel and location counts, you lack the "Y" (the result/outcome). Did you save money? Did you reduce transit time? Did you improve the quality of training?

**Version 1 (Safe improvement):** Managed logistics for a 600-person battalion training exercise, synchronizing operations across 3 geographically dispersed locations.
*What changed: Swapped "Coordinated" for "Managed" and "Synchronized" to highlight the complexity of cross-functional operations.*

**Version 2 (Stronger rewrite):** Orchestrated a 600-person battalion training exercise across 3 sites, reducing logistical lead time by [X]% through the implementation of a new supply tracking framework.
*What changed: Used "Orchestrated" to show high-level planning and added a placeholder for a quantifiable efficiency gain.*

**Version 3 (Strongest):** Architected the logistics strategy for a 600-person, multi-site training exercise, driving a [X]% increase in operational efficiency and saving $[Y] in projected resource costs.
*What changed: Positioned you as a strategist ("Architected") and tied the logistics work to tangible financial and operational outcomes.*

---

### Bullet 3
**Original:** Wrote standard operating procedures adopted by 2 other units

**Diagnosis:**
- WEAK VERB: "Wrote" is a low-impact verb that minimizes your role as a process innovator.
- BURIED LEAD: The fact that other units adopted your work is the most impressive part; it should be the headline.
- NO NUMBERS: How many people did this impact? How much time was saved?

**Version 1 (Safe improvement):** Developed standard operating procedures (SOPs) for unit operations that were subsequently adopted by 2 additional units to standardize performance.
*What changed: Used "Developed" and shifted the focus to the standardization of performance.*

**Version 2 (Stronger rewrite):** Standardized cross-unit operations by authoring new SOPs, resulting in a [X]% increase in process compliance across 3 total units.
*What changed: Quantified the impact and highlighted the cross-functional nature of the achievement.*

**Version 3 (Strongest):** Formalized and scaled best-in-class SOPs across 3 units, reducing onboarding time for [X] personnel by [Y]% and establishing a new standard for operational excellence.
*What changed: Framed the SOPs as a "scaling" activity and included placeholders for time-saved metrics to demonstrate direct value add.*

---

**Overall patterns:** You have strong raw data (leadership size, unit counts), but you are currently framing your experience as "doing tasks" rather than "driving outcomes." For MBB, shift your language from "maintaining" or "coordinating" to "optimizing," "architecting," and "driving results." Always look for the financial or efficiency-based "Y" in your XYZ formula.
