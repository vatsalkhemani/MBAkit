## Model output

## Your STAR Story

**Situation:**
Our B2B SaaS platform for enterprise admins was struggling with engagement, specifically regarding a high-effort "smart scheduling" feature I led that saw only 4% adoption six weeks post-launch despite significant pre-launch demand.

**Task:**
As the PM, I was responsible for diagnosing why a feature requested in five consecutive user research sessions failed to gain traction and determining the strategic path forward.

**Action:**
- Conducted 20 targeted follow-up discovery calls with non-adopting admins to uncover the disconnect between their initial requests and actual product usage.
- Analyzed the disparity between "stated pain" (what users said in interviews) and "felt pain" (the actual frequency of the scheduling bottleneck in their daily workflow).
- Identified that the feature solved a sporadic, low-priority edge case rather than a core daily friction point, rendering the ROI insufficient for enterprise workflows.
- Made the difficult decision to sunset the feature to prevent further technical debt and maintenance overhead.
- Authored a comprehensive post-mortem detailing the "stated vs. felt pain" gap, which was subsequently institutionalized as mandatory training for all new PMs to prevent future feature bloat.

**Result:**
While the feature didn't achieve its growth targets, the post-mortem prevented further wasted engineering cycles on low-value features and improved our product discovery framework, leading to a more rigorous validation process for new features. This taught me that customer obsession requires validating the *urgency* and *frequency* of a problem, not just the existence of it.

**Strength check:**
- Excellent use of "I" instead of "we."
- Strong "hard" decision-making: you killed your own project.
- The learning outcome is highly transferable and shows maturity.

**Gap flags:**
- **Quantify the cost:** You mention 4 months of building. How many engineers/designers were involved? Mentioning the "cost of failure" (e.g., "I decommissioned a feature that had consumed 1.5 FTEs over 4 months") makes the decision to kill it more impressive.
- **Specifics on the "Pivot":** Did you replace the feature with something else, or just kill it? If you have an example of how you used this new "stated vs. felt" framework to successfully launch a *different* feature later, add it as a final action bullet.
- **Data on the post-mortem:** Did it lead to a specific change in the product discovery process (e.g., "I introduced a mandatory 'Problem Frequency' scoring rubric")?

**Competency tags:** Customer Obsession, Product Sense, Ownership, Dealing with Failure.

**Likely follow-ups an interviewer would ask:**
1. "How did you handle the pushback from the engineering team who spent 4 months building this when you told them you were killing it?"
2. "What specific question do you now ask in user interviews to distinguish 'stated pain' from 'felt pain'?"
3. "Can you give an example of a feature you launched *after* this that successfully passed your new validation criteria?"

**Alternative framings:** This story also works for **"Tell me about a time you failed"** if you emphasize the emotional resilience required to kill your own project and the humility required to share the post-mortem publicly.
